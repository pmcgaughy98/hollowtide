# First route prototype: how to open it

Open `scenes/first_route_prototype.tscn` in Godot and choose **Run Current Scene** (F6). The project's normal Play button still opens `scenes/main.tscn`.

Move with WASD or the arrow keys. Press E beside the dispatcher, the crooked three-dot marker, the relay post, the river-height post, and the riverbank. Press J to open Field Notes or P to see your creature party outside dialogue; the same key or E closes the panel. Cinderhide begins in the first party slot. Captured creatures fill later slots. In the P panel, click a creature or focus it and press Enter to choose who travels with you; that creature enters the next battle. The panel updates when reopened. The notes separate observed route facts from unresolved questions before the final report. The route begins beside the dispatcher on the left. The sand trail bends uphill to the crooked marker, then descends past Cinderhide toward the ferry post. Carry the sealed pouch to the post, straighten the marker, optionally inspect the height post and listen at the river, then return to file a report. The undated high-water cuts are recorded as an observation; the scene makes no claim about what caused a past flood. The visible Cinderhide can be approached for the existing battle.

The objective and route board at the top track this one assignment. It resets when the scene restarts. The report and route board reflect the height marks if inspected before filing, and the player may still choose whether to include the uncertain river calls. Field Notes scroll when the observations outgrow the panel. The names and small landmark sprites are placeholders for review; creature art and the battle presentation use the current project assets. A first scenery pass adds an original Wayline office, cottonwoods, a bluff retaining wall, and a river-height marker. These are study props that give the route more shape without deciding its final art.
