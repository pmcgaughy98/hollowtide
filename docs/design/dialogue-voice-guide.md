# How Hollowtide talks (Claude, 2026-10-11)

Preston: the flood talk "sounds like caveman talk, like it's just describing the next action. ... I want them
to talk like someone from St. Joseph but bring more life into dialogue." Built by studying finished,
human-written games for structure only. No lines are taken from them.

## What the finished games do
- **Night in the Woods** (Infinite Fall, 2017; Scott Benson's 2018 GDC postmortem). They chose to make a game
  where "no one shuts up," with writing that does not efficiently point the player at the next destination.
  The characters are people first. The town is a place the writers knew (western and central Pennsylvania),
  and players from towns like it recognised it. Small sprites still carry big feeling when the lines are
  specific.
- **Explorers of Sky**, the partner on the beach. The partner is nervous and talks too much. It carries one
  personal object that tells you its dream before it says it. The talk is interrupted by something happening,
  and the plan comes from the interruption, not from a speech.
- **EarthBound and Stardew Valley**, small-town and rural neighbours. People talk about their own lives,
  chores, food and worries, in plain words with a dry joke now and then. Place shows through the small
  things they mention, not through dialect spelling.

## Rules for our lines
1. **React before you inform.** A scared character says "are you alive" before "the road is flooded." The facts
   come out sideways while they talk.
2. **Everybody wants something.** Put it under the line, not in it. The trainee wants to be a real runner and
   is ashamed of being turned away; "stick with me" carries that without saying it.
3. **Give them an object.** Something held, fixed or lost tells you who they are faster than a description.
4. **Let the world interrupt.** Thunder, the bank caving in, someone calling. The next step comes from what
   happens, not from someone announcing it.
5. **One joke per scene, even in a bad one.** People joke when they're scared. It makes the fear land.
6. **The player answers.** Short replies in the player's own voice that change the next line, so it is a
   conversation, not a briefing.
7. **Vary the rhythm.** A long rambling line, then a two-word one. Trailing off ("...And then there's you.").
8. **No narrator explaining feelings.** Narration says what you see and hear, never "You need a guide."

## St. Joseph, Missouri voice
Use lightly. One or two touches a scene, never phonetic spelling.
- "I'll tell you what," "about gave me a heart attack," "kinda," "real soft," "gotta," "ride it out."
- "Something just told me I needed to come down here tonight": the way people actually explain a gut feeling.
- "You guys" for a group; "the river's way up"; "it's coming up fast."
- Grammar can be loose in speech ("I seen you laying there") for characters it fits; keep it to one per scene.
- Plainspoken and friendly, not hillbilly. If a line sounds like a caricature, cut it.
