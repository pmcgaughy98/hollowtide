# The dungeon-crawl genre, synthesised, and what makes Hollowtide its own game (Claude, 2026-10-12)

Preston: "Utilize synthesis from all mystery dungeon style games too and synthesize the info and then improve and
refine everything so we can keep the foundation of what we have but build off mystery dungeon so much we don't even
have a chance for any companies to sue us." This file is the synthesis: the law as far as it can be checked, the
genre's shared skeleton across a dozen games, an audit of where Hollowtide still speaks in a borrowed voice, and a
ranked plan of refinements. Structure is taken from the games named; no art, text, names or data from any of them.

I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice. Before a public pitch or a store page, a games lawyer should read
the names, the recruiting rule and the store text (R8 below). What follows is what the public record says.

## 1. The legal frame, from the record

| What | Protected? | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The idea of a game, its name or title, the method of playing it | No, not by copyright | US Copyright Office, "Games": "The idea for a game is not protected by copyright. The name or title given to the game and the methods for playing it are not protected by copyright." (copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html) |
| Ideas, systems, methods of operation | No; only the way they are expressed | US Copyright Office FAQ, "What does copyright protect?" (copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html) |
| A game's look and feel: art, piece design, screen layout, animation, the audiovisual whole | Yes. Copying rules is allowed; copying the way the rules are shown is not | Tetris Holding v. Xio Interactive, 863 F.Supp.2d 394 (D.N.J. 2012): Mino copied no code or images, only Tetris's visual expression, and lost on copyright and trade dress |
| Specific mechanics, when patented | Yes, in Japan and the US | Nintendo and The Pokémon Company v. Pocketpair (Tokyo District Court, filed 2024): patents on aiming and throwing a capture object at a creature and on riding a creature; Pocketpair removed the thrown-sphere summon in 2025 (Hanson, "Playing the Patent Game", BC IPTF 2025; Pocketpair's own statement) |
| Names and titles as brands | Trademark, not copyright | "Mystery Dungeon" is Spike Chunsoft's series name (Nintendo's third-party copyright page lists "Chocobo's Mystery Dungeon" as licensed to and published by Spike Chunsoft). I could not pull the trademark register this session; treat the phrase as theirs regardless. "Pokémon" and its terms are Nintendo's and The Pokémon Company's |

What that means for us, in order of how much it matters:
1. **Never copy expression:** no sprites, portraits, tiles, music, text, names, UI drawn to match theirs. Hollowtide's
   art is painted in code or made from Preston's approved sources, its UI frames are Codex's own pixel 9-slices, its
   lines are in the house voice. Keep that discipline; it is the whole defence.
2. **Do not stack their signature expression even when each piece is a free idea.** Tetris v. Xio lost on the sum of
   borrowed choices. Hollowtide should not read, side by side with Explorers of Sky, as the same screens with the
   names filed off. Section 3 lists the pieces that still do.
3. **Avoid their marks in our words:** never "Mystery Dungeon" as our genre label (say "dungeon crawl" or "turn-based
   dungeon crawl"), never Pokémon terms in-game or in marketing.
4. **Watch patented mechanics, not genre ideas:** throwing an object to capture a creature and riding creatures are
   the two the public record names. Hollowtide has neither; recruiting is a beaten wild creature asking to join,
   which is older than Pokémon Mystery Dungeon (Azure Dreams's familiars, Shiren's companions, Torneko's) and is not
   a thrown capture. R8 asks a lawyer to confirm.

## 2. The genre, across games (structure only)

Sourced where marked; the rest is my working knowledge of the games and should be checked before it is quoted.

| Game | The run | Between runs | On defeat | Companions | Its signature |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rogue (1980) and NetHack | one descent, random floors, hunger, unknown items | nothing: permadeath | the run ends | none | permadeath, identification |
| Torneko (1993, the first Chunsoft "Mystery Dungeon") | the Rogue run, friendlier | a town and a shop that grows with what you bring back | lose carried items, keep the town | none | the hub that grows |
| Shiren the Wanderer (1995 on) | the Rogue run; **level resets to 1 on leaving a dungeon by any exit** (Shiren 5 manual, Spike Chunsoft) | villages, a storehouse, blacksmith, rescue passwords | items and money lost unless rescued | companions in later games | the storehouse and rescues |
| Azure Dreams (1997) | a tower; eggs hatch into familiars that **keep experience after leaving** (GameFAQs guide) | **the town is built up between runs** with what you bring home | familiars keep their growth | familiars | monster-raising plus town-building |
| Chocobo's Dungeon (1997 on) | the run with jobs | a village | lose items | a buddy | jobs on a Chocobo |
| Pokémon Mystery Dungeon (2005 on) | the run with Belly (hunger), IQ, Tactics, Monster Houses | a guild with a morning briefing, a Job Bulletin Board, Kecleon shops, Wonder Mail | **"levels and moves stay, half the money and some items go"** (Explorers of Sky manual, Nintendo); rescue by other players | the whole team; beaten wilds ask to join | the team of creatures and the story |
| Etrian Mystery Dungeon (2015) | the run with a party of classes | a town with guild and forts | lose items | a party | forts that hold floors |
| Tangledeep (2018), One Way Heroics (2012) | the run; One Way Heroics: the world's edge chases you | a hub with persistent unlocks | varies | pets, companions | a moving wall of darkness (One Way Heroics) |
| Crypt of the NecroDancer (2015) | the run on a beat | a lobby with unlocks | the run ends | none | rhythm |
| Hades (2020), Dead Cells (2018) | real-time runs | a home where everyone reacts to your death | the run ends, progression stays | none | death as story |

**What is genre-wide and free to use:** a grid, turns, random floors with stairs, rooms and halls, a clock on the run
(hunger, light, a wall), items in a bag, traps, unknown items, shops, a hub between runs, companions, a run that ends
with carried things lost and growth kept, a boss at the bottom, recruiting what you beat, a den of waiting enemies.

**What is one game's signature expression and should not be ours:** PMD's Belly, IQ, Tactics names, Monster House
(also Chunsoft's), Wonder Mail, Kecleon, rescue missions, the morning briefing with a cheer, "You fainted" with the
money halved; Shiren's level reset and storehouse; Azure Dreams's eggs; NecroDancer's beat.

## 3. Hollowtide audited

**Already ours, and the core of the identity:** the river that rises and the Hollow that rose out of it; a courier
company (the Wayline) and runners carrying parcels; the lantern as the run's clock (light, not hunger); the pond stone
and the Growth Shrine; the six essences and their chart; Grok's creature rules; the crew (Grubbit, Stillwade,
Wickglow, Muskin, Slipjaw, Rinsepaw) with jobs and job objects; the painted dungeons; the house voice; the faint flow
with the partner's word first (section 4 of the faint screen notes).

**Genre-wide, keep:** the grid and turns, floors and stairs, the bag, traps, scrolls that teach moves, the hub town
with a job board, recruits that ask to join, a boss floor, the run that ends in a faint with the bag lost.

**Still in a borrowed voice (names or beats), change:**

| Now | Why it reads as theirs | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| "IQ" and "IQ skills" | PMD's exact system name | **Sense**: what a creature has picked up on the river (the skills keep their ids) |
| "Tactics" and its order names | PMD's exact menu | **Orders**, in a courier's words (section 5, R2) |
| "Monster House" | Chunsoft's term in Shiren and PMD | **a nest** ("A nest! They're all awake.") |
| "Rally Seed" that revives the leader once | PMD's Reviver Seed by another name | keep the item, call it **Second Wind** (a root you chew; the id `rally_seed` stays) |
| "X fainted!" | Pokémon's word for a knockout, used everywhere | **"X went down."** for wilds and partners; the leader is **carried back** |
| the guild, the Guildmaster, "guild trial" | PMD's guild with a Guildmaster | the **Wayline** (already the name), its **Postmaster** (Starward), and a **first run** instead of a trial; story flags keep their ids |
| the morning roll call with a cheer | Explorers of Sky's morning briefing and cheer, beat for beat | **mail call**: Wayling sorts the morning's parcels and hands out routes; Muskin still late (R3) |
| "evolution" in player-facing text | Pokémon's word; Digimon's too | **growth** and **next form** in the UI (the Growth Shrine already says it); code keeps `evolves_into` |
| the dialogue portrait above the box's left corner | the Sky layout | our frame, with the portrait set into the box's left end (R7) |

## 4. What to push, so the distance grows on its own

- **Deliveries, not explorations.** Every run is a parcel: a destination floor or a creature to reach, delivery
  conditions (dry, before the lantern dies, unopened), and pay that depends on the condition at arrival. The Job
  Board becomes a postbag.
- **The river as the clock and the terrain.** Tide dungeons flood a row of tiles every so many turns; high ground,
  fords, currents that carry items and creatures a tile downstream. The lantern already punishes slow runs; water
  punishes the wrong path.
- **The town grows with routes** (Torneko's and Azure Dreams's idea, in our frame): reopening a washed-out route
  unlocks a service or a crew member; deliveries pay for the Hall's rooms. Nothing bought with coins that drop from
  monsters; everything earned by carrying.
- **Growth stays ours:** the Growth Shrine, conditions and the stone, not levels alone.
- **The crew reacts to how runs end** (Hades's idea): one line per crew member after a faint, keyed to who beat you.

## 5. The refinement plan, ranked (each one is one session, ending in something that runs)

1. **R1, the words (this session):** nest, Sense, Orders, Second Wind, "went down", carried back, Postmaster, first
   run, growth. Player-facing strings only; ids and story flags unchanged; checks updated and run.
2. **R2, Orders in a courier's words:** the T key cycles orders named for how runners move: "Stay close", "Spread
   out", "Push on", "Hold" (names to Preston).
3. **R3, mail call:** rewrite the lower-floor morning scene as parcel sorting; Wayling calls routes, not names.
   Done 2026-10-12 (`scripts/town/guild_crew.gd`): routes claimed by whoever runs them, Muskin late and handed the
   Warren road, the runners' route from the Postmaster's morning note, no cheer; the crew peel off to work.
4. **R4, delivery conditions on jobs:** parcel types and pay by condition at arrival.
   Done 2026-10-12 (`scripts/town/jobs.gd`, `dungeon.gd`): a third request kind, a delivery; the parcel rides in the
   bag (lost with it on a faint, and the delivery comes off the slate); the condition (before the lantern goes
   out, within N turns, without springing a trap) is judged at the client's feet; met, double reward and more
   rank. "Dry" waits for R5's water. Numbers are proposals for Grok.
5. **R5, rising water on Tide floors:** one flooding floor in The Hollow's valley.
   Done 2026-10-12 (`dungeon.gd`, the rising water section): on The Hollow's valley floor the water comes up from
   the lowest floor row every 10 turns, never past the stairs or the start; each rise carries whoever stands in it
   and whatever lies in it a tile downstream (Tide creatures swim); wet feet cost lantern. Drawn as a translucent
   band with a water line; the Floor page reports it. Numbers are proposals for Grok. `tools/debug/water_check.gd`.
6. **R6, the town grows with routes:** one service unlocked by a reopened route.
   Done 2026-10-12 (`scripts/town/storage.gd`, `hub_town.gd`): the Storage is shut behind a note until Rootbank
   Warren's crew are beaten (the river road runs past their den); then it keeps items between runs, a faint
   loses the bag and never the store, parcels are for carrying not keeping, the bag's kinds cap holds on the way
   out. Nothing bought. `tools/debug/storage_check.gd`.
7. **R7, the dialogue frame:** portrait set into the box, our arrangement.
   Done 2026-10-12 (`scripts/ui/ui_style.gd`, `dialogue`): the portrait is set into the box's left end with its
   frame straddling the top edge, the name tab beside it, the line to its right; one function, so every box in
   the game changed at once (town, rooms, dungeon, the flood, the faint screen, the opening). Stills in
   `docs/design/dialogue-frame-2026-10-12/`.
8. **R8, a lawyer's read** of the names, the recruit rule and the store text before the pitch.
