# The antagonist crew: proposal (Claude, 2026-10-10)

Preston: "the warden will actually be second boss, like how in Mystery Dungeon Koffing and Zubat were
first bosses technically. So we'll need to make an antagonist line; we'll make their first forms the
first bosses, not hard at all, like Mystery Dungeon." Preston chose the **opossum and mosquito** pair,
as a **rival courier crew**. **Preston approved the names (2026-10-10)** and asked Claude to pick the moves
from the synthesis, so each new creature fills a gap the roster still has. Numbers stay drafts for Grok.

## What we take from Explorers of Sky (structure only)
- **A crew of three:** two unevolved grunts and a leader who is the evolved form of one of their
  lines. The grunts are the first boss, together, in the first dungeon.
- **The first boss is a lesson, not a wall:** low health, one simple trick each, no escort, a short
  scene of banter. It teaches the boss room (the sealed stairs, the scene, the fight) with little risk.
- **They come back:** cowardly and comic when beaten ("The boss'll hear about this!"), they turn up
  again in town and later dungeons; the leader is a real fight further on.
- **The second dungeon gets the first serious boss** (for us, the Quietwater Warden).

## The crew: a rival courier outfit (working name: the Shortcut Crew)
Cut-rate runners who poach Job Board requests, cut corners, and rob honest couriers' deliveries. They
are why the Job Board posts warnings. In town they loiter by the board and sneer at the player's rank.

## Art rule for the evolutions (Preston, 2026-10-10)
"Make sure their evolutions look like monsters, not animals from Earth." Mirekit and Whinet start close
to an opossum and a mosquito, but Scavrind, Gutterking, Siphonfly and Bogdrake must read as Hollowtide
monsters: invented anatomy, rot growths and fungus, extra limbs or wings, glowing marks, armour, shapes
no real animal has. The animal stays as a hint (the bare tail, the needle), never as the whole design.

## Line 11: the opossum (Blight, then Blight/Murk). The crew's leader is its final form.
A Missouri river scavenger: bare tail, pointed snout, grey-white fur stained by the mud it digs in.
Its trick is **playing dead**, a real opossum behaviour.

A marsh biter from the river bottoms, all whine and needle. Small, fast, irritating.

## The first boss: Mirekit and Whinet (first dungeon, the Hollow, B3F's top floor)
**The story beat:** on the first run, the Shortcut Crew snatches the parcel the courier was given
(the first Job Board request, or the tutorial delivery). The team chases them down the Hollow. On the
boss floor they are cornered, and they fight.

**The scene (the Warden scene's structure, shorter):** the team walks in; the two are arguing over the
parcel; a "!" when they notice the team; Mirekit brags, Whinet whines; streak wipe; fight. After the
fight: Mirekit flops over playing dead, Whinet drags it off ("The boss'll hear about this!"), and the
parcel is left behind.

**Easy on purpose (numbers for Grok; the Warden is 3x a wild creature's health with an escort):**
- Each about 1.5x a wild stage-1's health, damage 0.7x, no escort.
- **Mirekit, Play Dead:** at half health it flops over for two turns (takes half damage), then gets up
  with a cheap shot at whoever is beside it. The lesson: a boss can bait you.
- **Whinet, Drain Bite:** a bite that heals it a little. The lesson: focus the healer.
- A level 4 or 5 team should win without items.

## The story so far (Preston, 2026-10-10)
1. **The first dungeon, the Hollow:** the Shortcut Crew steals the courier's first parcel. The team chases
   them down; Mirekit and Whinet are the first boss.
2. **The second dungeon, the risen structure:** a dungeon, or a structure leading to one, **rose out of
   the river**, with a **stone pathway** out to it. It is full of mystery, and it is the team's **first
   assigned mission**: it is expected to be safe enough for rookies, which is why they are sent. It
   is not: its heart is the Quietwater Warden's pond garden.
3. **The artifact:** something lies in the pond. After the Warden and its Listeners faint, **it rises
   from the water**. It is the item that starts the journey (as the partner's relic does in Explorers of
   Sky, the one the first bosses stole). It is why the Listener line is so protective: they were
   guarding it.

Proposals for Preston: what the artifact is and its name (a disc of river glass that hums? a stone
with the Wayline mark? something that answers the soul from the prologue?), and what the risen
structure is called.

## Moves (Claude, from the synthesis; numbers are drafts for Grok)
What the roster lacks after Springvault: nothing **cuts healing**, and nothing **dives the backline and
lives** (Scourmaw ambushes but has no sustain). The crew fills both, and fights the way thieves fight.

**The opossum line: the Saboteur.** Rot that **halves healing** (the answer to Springvault and Seep),
fake deaths, theft. Evolves by surviving: `took` (damage it takes for the team).

| Form | Basic | Moves (level) | Passive | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mirekit | Grub Bite (front, Blight) | 1 Play Dead: flops for 2 turns taking half damage, then a cheap shot at whoever is beside it; 3 Rot Spit (line 3, **Blighted**: healing on the target halved for 3 turns) | | |
| Scavrind | Grub Bite | evolve Pickpocket (front: takes the target's shield, or an item if it is wild); 6 Tail Hook (pulls a foe 2 tiles to it, the hooking tail) | Scavenger: heals a little when a foe nearby faints | |
| Gutterking | Grub Bite | evolve Rot Heap (burst 2, Blighted on everything in it) | Scavenger | **Last Laugh** (arena): plays dead; the first foe to come close sets off a rot burst that Blights and knocks back |

Body: claw, jaw, hooktail (its own part; Tail Hook needs it).

**The mosquito line: the Harrier.** Hit-and-run that **drains** to stay alive on the backline.
Evolves by striking from behind: `flank`.

| Form | Basic | Moves (level) | Passive | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whinet | Needle Jab (front, Gale) | 1 Drain Bite (front, Blight: heals itself half the damage); 3 Buzz Dart (dash 3, then it can step back) | | |
| Siphonfly | Needle Jab | evolve Siphon (front, a bigger drain); 6 Whine (burst 2: foes' wind-ups take a turn longer) | Engorged: after a drain its next hit is stronger | |
| Bogdrake | Needle Jab | evolve Skewer Dive (dash 5 over everyone, hits the last foe in the line) | Engorged | **Swarm Season** (arena): splits into a cloud of needles that drains everything it passes and reforms beside the weakest foe |

Body: needle, wings, sixlegs (needle and wings are its own; Drain Bite and Siphon need the needle).

**New effects the dungeon does not have yet** (built in the boss slice, since the first fight needs two
of them): Blighted (healing halved), Play Dead, Drain (heal from damage dealt), Pickpocket's steal.
Until each exists, the move waits (the WAITING list), like Burrow and Glass Guard.

**Matchups this would add** (from the essence proposal; the new essences stay neutral in the game's
chart until Grok sets them): Blight beats Bloom and Tide, so the crew is strong against Tallowroot's line
and the Warden's water, and weak to Stone and Ember (Kithflare and Cinderhide answer it), which makes
the first boss a fair lesson for a Kithflare or Cinderhide starter.

## The order to build it (one slice per session)
1. **This proposal.** Preston picks or changes names and looks; Grok sets the kits.
2. **Art:** Mirekit and Whinet first (the boss pair), in the dungeon size set with walk, hurt and sleep
   frames, then portraits. Retro Diffusion, with a cost estimate before any spending.
3. **Two dungeons:** the Hollow (first, the crew's boss floor) and a second dungeon for the Warden's
   pond garden. Dungeon data moves from single constants to a small list (name, floors, wild pool,
   boss, boss scene). The Warden's scene and pond move with it unchanged.
4. **The crew's boss scene and fight** (the duo, Play Dead, Drain Bite).
5. **The crew in town** by the Job Board, and the leader's later fight.
