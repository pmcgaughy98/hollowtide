## Original prompt supplied to Gemini (retained for audit)

You are the research lead and animation production planner for Hollowtide, an original Godot monster-collector game. Research **only the nine Ember forms whose art has already been generated**:

1. Cinderhide → Clinkerhide → Ironvent: a charcoal/lava-rock reptile line with vents, integrated clinker hide, weight-bearing claws, and increasing control over heat.
2. Kithflare → Wayburn → Coalcrest: an earless, four-legged reptilian relay courser line. Its broad triangular skull, slit eye, angular pale throat mark, rust zigzag, split counterweight tail, three-toed feet, flush lower-rib heat seams, and changing gait/mass are identity anchors. It is not a dog, otter, wolf, horse, or a creature wearing armor. Derive its abilities from locomotion, compressed heat, traction, momentum, endurance, heat sensing, and its role in Hollowtide's relay ecology; do not default to generic fireballs or imported franchise moves. Treat these as starting hypotheses to validate against the actual art and lore.
3. Fusern → Slagglass → Vitrecrag: a six-legged kiln arthropod line with one continuous underside, tucked head, grown layered carapace, internal furnace, and widening load-bearing legs. Slagglass opens its vitrified shell into attached overlapping shelves. Vitrecrag is a massive field creature compressed for battle, with stacked dark shell plates and an orange-gold furnace/glass chamber. Its **molten golden-orange glass is a physical weapon**, not a generic flame sticker: it can gather, extrude, cast, fire, and cool molten glass in ways consistent with its body. Study how to make a sustained **molten-glass beam** and a distinct **cannon-like shot** feel massive and dangerous. The latter may borrow the abstract rhythm of a heavy charged projectile, but do not copy Diaboromon or any commercial design, pose, sound, or effect.

First inspect the supplied images and current code. For every observed visual feature, cite the exact local filename. Label facts seen in files, reasonable inferences, and new proposals separately. Use the roster and world-bible as design context while noting which names, physiology, and lore details remain draft. Build coherent ecology and evolution: where the heat comes from, which structures make a move possible, what each stage gains or cannot yet do, and what using the ability costs or changes in behavior. Moves should visibly originate from the anatomy, abilities, temperament, and local lore, with a different visual grammar for each of the three lines.

Research a practical **full-body 2D pixel animation system** that can cover idle, locomotion, anticipation, melee, guard, interruption, hit reaction, ranged charge/release, recovery, and a large creature's heavy movement. Compare (A) Node2D/Sprite2D cutouts with AnimationPlayer, (B) Skeleton2D or bone deformation, (C) hand-drawn frame replacement, and (D) a hybrid. The present Cinderhide and Clinkerhide pilots only move a head by one source pixel; they prove exact visible-layer extraction, not full-body readiness. Explain how to cut existing approved fronts and backs into torso, jaw/head, near/far limbs, tail, shell plates, furnace/vent overlays, and other meaningful parts **without altering their visible rest pose**, and how to paint the hidden joint pixels that cropping cannot recover. Give part boundaries, pivot logic, depth order, foot locks, subpixel/nearest-filter risks, front/back consistency, and a test at the actual 1152×648 battle resolution. Address a four-legged reptile and six-legged stacked-shell arthropod separately. State clearly which actions require redrawn pixels instead of rigid transforms.

Design a repeatable move-animation pipeline, rather than relying on a static projectile icon plus generic particles. Separate (1) creature body action, (2) emitted material/effect, (3) target contact and aftermath, and (4) camera, hitstop, sound, and UI feedback. Explain when an effect should be a keyed hand-drawn sequence, a segmented beam, animated mesh/shader, particles, or a combination. Preserve crisp native pixel clusters and the exact flat magenta source/export workflow; most existing creature grids are 100×100, while Vitrecrag's approved form is 128×128. Do not impose a universal 64×64 grid, fixed palette ceiling, transparent output from Opal, or automatic pixel-exact AI preservation. Recommend a generation workflow that produces rig-friendly base art and per-part/pose references without making visible cut seams or forcing a new design. Include staged Opal prompts and human cleanup steps that fit limited generation quotas.

Ground your Godot recommendations in the current implementation: battle_screen.gd chooses drawn attack frames only when the move has them and the attacker is the foe or lacks a back sprite. Player back-view attacks otherwise take the projectile/effect path. The current drawn attack loop uses one frame duration, a penultimate contact frame, and a fixed small lunge. Vitrecrag's beam and cannon scenes currently present projectile/impact sprites with generic ember particle Trail and Burst; they are not yet a continuous beam or a materially distinct cannon release. Propose data and scene changes needed to support one action timeline for either front or back view, with named anticipation, release, contact, recovery, interruption, and cleanup events. Avoid brittle claims about Godot 4.7.2 APIs; verify each proposed class, method, import/filter setting, and animation behavior in official Godot documentation.

Deliver all of the following:

A. A one-page production decision: which actions use rigging, which use redrawn frames, and when to combine them, with quality and labor tradeoffs.
B. **Nine creature production cards**, one per generated form. For each: observed anatomical anchors, lore/physiology hypothesis, silhouette and mass, possible rig parts/pivots, one signature idle, one locomotion or weight shift, one hit reaction, and a **four-move candidate kit**. For each move state how the body starts it, what material or force appears, the contact shape, and why this form can perform it. Distinguish stage upgrades; do not make Stage II a larger Stage I or Stage III a pile of added armor.
C. Three detailed action storyboards, at minimum: Ironvent Cinder Claw, a Kithflare-line relay/heat-momentum move selected from its actual art, and Vitrecrag molten-glass beam **plus** cannon shot. For Vitrecrag, show furnace charge, opening or nozzle anatomy, material trajectory, color/temperature/viscosity stages, impact/cooling, and recovery. The beam must read as sustained connection; the cannon as one heavy launched mass. Include approximate frame/hold timing and contact events, with room for visual judgment.
D. A rig blueprint for **Cinderhide front/back, Clinkerhide front/back, and Vitrecrag front/back**: suggested cut hierarchy, pivots, needed underpaint, likely sprite-only redraws, and a rest-pose reconstruction check. Treat Clinkerhide back as a candidate awaiting Preston's approval, and do not claim Vitrecrag's current Godot texture paths load until checked.
E. A Godot integration sketch naming the current scripts/resources that would change, a small data schema for action cues and per-view clips, and one reversible pilot sequence. Preserve damage timing, continuous HP, battle HUD, capture/faint transitions, existing creature art, and frame fallback while piloting.
F. A Forge input/output contract for rig-friendly creatures and for NEW/COMPLETE attack sheets; propose precise, copy-ready prompts for a Vitrecrag beam body action, Vitrecrag cannon body action, and the selected Kithflare-line action. Include a rejection/revision table for perspective flips, fused limbs, foot sliding, palette drift, magenta fringes, tiny 8-bit-looking effects, and attacks that never change silhouette.
G. A prioritized build order and measurable acceptance gates at native battle scale, with generation count and manual-pixel-work estimates. State the smallest next pilot that would prove the pipeline before expanding to all nine forms.
H. A source ledger with direct links, access dates, and separate labels for sourced practice, direct project observation, inference, and proposal. Prioritize official Godot 4.x documentation and firsthand animator/developer breakdowns. Do not reproduce protected sprite sheets, franchise moves, or proprietary designs. End with at most five specific questions for Preston that materially affect art direction.

Do not modify project files or declare new lore canon. Return a research report that Codex and Claude can turn into concrete tasks and that Preston can review creature by creature.
