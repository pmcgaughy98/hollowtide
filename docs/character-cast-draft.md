# Hollowtide character cast

**Proposal draft, 2026-09-26.** These roles and sample lines are fictional working material, not approved names or final dialogue. They do not depict real St. Joseph or Elwood residents. Read this with the [region atlas](region-atlas-draft.md) and [far-bank slice](far-bank-slice-draft.md) after it is placed in the project.

| Role | First useful appearance | What they want | Player action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wayline dispatcher | First route board | Reports another runner can trust | Separate observation from assumption |
| Ferry-post keeper | After the empty post | A crossing mark that still means something | Hear their explanation directly |
| Far-bank route maintainer | Repair day across the river | Local warnings to stay dated and visible | Inspect raised and lower paths |
| Story-house caretaker | Highbank collection | Keep overlooked records available | Label an object with what its record supports |
| Mill apprentice | Mill Cut | Account for materials and missed parcels | Trace a parcel's physical route |
| Field naturalist | Ridge and old channel | Record creature behavior carefully | Compare dated sightings and calls |

## Voices in play

**Dispatcher.** Brisk and willing to correct the board. Their first-route response changes with the player's report. Later, they must warn travelers without claiming to know Hollowtide's cause. “Tell me where you stood and what you heard. Someone else has to walk by your mark.”

**Keeper.** The dry slot, return mark, and dated notices establish their routine before the player meets them. Their opening absence needs an ordinary, character-specific explanation. The player hears it directly before adopting anyone else's theory. “If I cannot stand behind a mark today, I will not leave yesterday's mark in its place.”

**Maintainer.** They know who needs the raised track and lower cut. Their immediate concern is safe travel, not winning an argument about an old flood. They speak for their own work, not every far-bank resident. “That board was right when it was written. Check the ground before you copy it.”

**Caretaker.** A dramatic fictional outlaw exhibit brings visitors through the door, while work ledgers and family donations remain less visible. The player can label an object with what its record supports and leave an uncertain attribution visible. “They ask for the outlaw. I can show them the hands that kept this place running too.”

**Apprentice.** They know which loads passed a scale and which receipts were copied. They help reconstruct a missing message's physical route before anyone blames a person, machine, or creature. “If it never crossed this scale, start looking before the river.”

**Naturalist.** They study calls, tracks, and habitat changes. They compare observations from two places but do not announce an answer from one sighting. Creatures are living neighbors, not just battle data. “Two calls can mean two creatures. Write down the distance before you decide.”

## Shared arc

The first route teaches carrying, observing, and reporting. The far bank shows that route marks affect people whose conditions differ from Highbank's. The story house and mill reveal work hidden behind a familiar tale. The naturalist helps test explanations without replacing human testimony. These people may disagree about evidence and still work together on a safer route.

Keep lines short in play. Let movement, props, the board, and creature behavior carry part of each scene. No single character explains the whole region.
