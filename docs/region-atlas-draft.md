# Hollowtide Region Atlas

**Proposal draft, 2026-09-26.** This extends the [living bible](world-bible.md) and [20-line creature roster](creature-roster-draft.md). Area labels and character roles below are working proposals, not approved names or final plot reveals. The real St. Joseph, Missouri research remains in [field notes](research/st-joseph-field-notes.md); the setting and people here are fictional.

## Geographic spine

Highbank sits above the river. The courier road descends through a bluff switchback to the Old Ferry Ward and its landing. Upland springs and grass openings lie behind town; a rail and mill corridor cuts across the lower industrial terraces. The far bank has its own homes, fields, and flood defenses. Downstream, an older channel holds slow water and places where messages are hard to place.

This gives the player three readable relationships: **height** (safe bluff versus exposed bank), **connection** (courier, ferry, rail, and creature paths), and **memory** (the route people use now versus the one records and stories describe). It is not a street map of St. Joseph.

| Area | What the player does there | Texture to build toward |
|---|---|---|
| Highbank | Receive assignments, choose a partner, return with reports, hear competing accounts. | Layered streets, a practical dispatch room, old route marks, a small story house. |
| Bluff switchback and ridge | Learn route care, meet visible wild creatures, follow spring and prairie habitats. | Loess-colored cuts, oak shade, grass openings, dry wind, short sightlines around bends. |
| Old Ferry Ward | Deliver to the quiet post, listen at the river, find which crossings still work. | An underused landing, wet timber, high-water stains, a broad river that feels close but difficult to reach. |
| Far bank and low fields | Meet people affected differently by river risk; compare reports without deciding who is right for them. | Raised thresholds, field tracks, repair crews, shelter routes, shared labor. |
| Mill Cut and rail bed | Follow materials and messages through grain, kiln, and switching yards. | Elevators, conveyors, loading doors, rail ballast, warm machinery, working neighborhoods. |
| Old Channel | Investigate displaced calls and changed creature behavior late in the journey. | Still pools, cut-off bends, silt bars, reeds, broken route markers. |

The first playable scene covers only the dispatch-to-river-and-back loop. The [far-bank slice draft](far-bank-slice-draft.md) sketches the next work assignment after that scene is reviewed. The other areas are production directions for later slices, not a request to build six maps at once.

## The first story arc

1. **An ordinary delivery becomes an unanswered one.** A new runner delivers a sealed pouch. The relay post is empty, its return mark is missing, and two calls may be heard near the river. The player reports what they actually observed.
2. **The far bank answers back.** The player's report sends another runner, then opens a visit across the water. Residents and maintainers describe present-day route problems and flood preparations in their own terms. Their accounts should be distinct rather than a town-wide verdict.
3. **The ridge changes the question.** Springs, dry slopes, and creature habitats show that the signal problem is not confined to the river edge. A creature's field behavior can help distinguish a living call from an old echo, but it cannot solve every human disagreement.
4. **Working records complicate the legend.** At Mill Cut, route logs, material deliveries, and people's labor reveal how messages moved before the current network. A fictional outlaw story at Highbank's story house can be examined as civic folklore: who preserved the exhibit, whose work is absent, and why visitors remember one dramatic ending. This is not a collectible outlaw creature or a retelling of Jesse James as a game character.
5. **Reopen a connection.** At the old channel, the player has enough observations to help decide which fictional route to mark safe first and what uncertainty to put on the public board. The nature of Hollowtide and the cause of the fictional region's old flood remain open design decisions. A first game ending can resolve the courier and community problem without declaring an unverified real-world flood claim true.

The central player action is to **carry, observe, and report**. Battles and captures support exploration and partnership; they do not replace listening to people or reading the place.

## People whose roles make the region feel lived in

The [character cast draft](character-cast-draft.md) gives these roles voice, a playable action, and a first appearance. These are role sketches, not named characters:

- **Dispatcher:** teaches useful, precise reporting. Their response to the two-call report is the first visible consequence of the player's wording.
- **Ferry-post keeper:** absent during the opening delivery. Their reason should be practical and character-specific when revealed; the empty post alone is not evidence of a supernatural disappearance.
- **Far-bank route maintainer:** knows the cost of keeping a crossing open and can challenge Highbank's assumptions without becoming a spokesperson for every resident.
- **Story-house caretaker:** tends a famous local exhibit and knows which records were moved, lost, or sold. They can hold pride and skepticism at once.
- **Mill worker or apprentice:** understands the physical journey of grain, fuel, parcels, and recorded messages; their work supplies clues and everyday stakes.
- **Field naturalist:** helps the player interpret creature behavior while remaining honest about what cannot be known from one sighting.

Avoid making any one person a simple mouthpiece for a real St. Joseph resident or a real historical victim. The story-house figure and outlaw legend are fictional.

## Creature ecology for the current 20-line roster

This table places **Stage I** forms in readable habitats and suggests where a line's later growth belongs. It does not change the roster's names, essence chart, stat values, or evolution triggers. All names are draft. Starters can be partner choices even when their wild relatives live elsewhere.

| Line | First clear habitat | Growth and field-role direction |
|---|---|---|
| Cinderhide → Clinkerhide → Ironvent | Warm bluff cracks and worked stone near Highbank. | A heat-warning guardian; later forms hold ground rather than becoming a generic fire dragon. |
| Kithflare → Wayburn → Coalcrest | Dry relay tracks between stations. | Handoff signals, bursts of speed, and recovery after long runs. |
| Fusern → Slagglass → Vitrecrag | Kiln walls and warm material yards at Mill Cut. | Heat shelter and layered protection; not a living tool or factory accident. |
| Rimefin → Riftfin → Scourmaw | Fast river seams and scoured shallows. | From quick current reader to patient channel predator. |
| Cutbank Listener → Bend Caller → Quietwater Warden | Cutbanks, then the older channel. | Recognizes calls and echoes; later guidance can reveal a safer route. |
| Siltcrank → Millcurrent → Braidwake | Slow eddies and braided shallows. | Maintains pools and reshapes current, without literal machine anatomy. |
| Marlward → Terraceward → Bluffmantle | Loess and limestone ledges. | Stabilizes footing and shelters smaller creatures on steep ground. |
| Flintram → Mortarback → Cutbank Bastion | Exposed eroding banks. | Protects a fragile slope; its presence can signal a dangerous cut. |
| Ballastak → Switchhold → Deepcut | Rail-bed gravel and service cuts. | Burrowing reveals hidden paths and material layers. |
| Troughspine → Archroot → Springvault | Upland springs and shaded seep lines. | Follows water through stone without becoming an architectural object. |
| Skirr → Scudder → Stormveer | Ridge grass and gust corridors. | Reads wind shifts before travelers feel them. |
| Dustkite → Waywhistle → Highsignal | Bluff air above route markers. | Carries short calls across bends; shares signal ecology with, but does not replace, couriers. |
| Canvaswing → Windcut → Galeshear | Open floodplain thermals. | Long glides between banks and fields. |
| Preostmar → Oathfen → Oxbowarchon | Sheltered river bends, oxbow pools, and old flood channels. | Carries a living shell basin that can shelter small creatures and seed stores during high water. |
| Cotterseed → Canopyvein → Floodcrown | Floodplain shade and raised roots. | Provides temporary shelter when water changes familiar paths. |
| Milletusk → Threshback → Gleanhorn | Grainfield edges, not inside a working machine. | A grazer that follows spilled seed and field cycles. |
| Masonsward → Thimblecomb → Combwright | Town walls, garden plots, and dry crevices. | Pollination and small-scale building; useful to people without being owned by an industry. |
| Hushmire → Lullfen → Tarnreach | Quiet wetland pools. | Reduces sound and calms habitats; Hollow does not mean evil. |
| Echolarva → Stillshade → Nullchorus | Still backwaters of the old channel. | Reflects and absorbs calls, making observation difficult rather than automatically hostile. |
| Mutecho → Unanswered → Unspoken | Abandoned route posts and the old channel. | Mimics message patterns; its existence invites careful reporting, not an instant mystery answer. |

No Stage III form needs to roam the opening route. Its first appearance should feel earned through a partner's growth, a habitat expedition, or a local account that gives the creature a place in the world.

## Art and map implications

Use the imported CC0 grass, sand, bank, and water sheet as generic terrain material for the first route. Build signature route markers, courier stations, neighborhood silhouettes, flood marks, and creature sprites as Hollowtide originals. The first route's SVG landmarks are placeholders. Keep creature pixel density and grounded proportions consistent with the approved Forge conversions; avoid bright toy-like environmental props that make them look imported from another game.

For each new area, finish one small walkable loop with a purpose, one habitat encounter, one person to speak with, and one return consequence before adding another map. This keeps the region's connections visible in play, not only in this document.
