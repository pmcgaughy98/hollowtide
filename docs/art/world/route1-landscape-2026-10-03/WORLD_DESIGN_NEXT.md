# Current world design checkpoint
The user's active goal is a Hollowtide game world and map levels capable of competing with Nexomon and Coromon. The St. Joseph region supplies geography, materials, history and neighborhood identity; Hollowtide uses its own names, creatures and fiction.

## Existing first journey
Fictional west-bank hometown → dispatch lane → levee entrance → quiet river route and old landing → complete bridge crossing → east-bank riverfront/courier branch → future city sectors.

Route 1 should remain a compact first journey. Its purpose is to teach movement, observation, courier work, an encounter and a meaningful river crossing. A larger rectangle of filler is not the next target.

## Next implementation order
1. **Levee and route readability.** Give the road a clear raised-earth form with visible grassy slopes and coherent entrances. Replace the remaining long right-angle path/terrace joins with authored contours while keeping every quest approach legible. Ground the old height post and landing in that geography.
2. **A purposeful optional loop.** Add a small discoverable space with an authored observation or interaction that returns to the main trail. Coordinate any rewards/inventory persistence with Claude's state ownership rather than adding a parallel state system.
3. **Map connections.** Build the hometown and riverfront as actual connected levels. Define arrival positions, camera bounds, collision edges and return behavior. The existing city directions board should point toward playable exits as sectors are implemented.
4. **District silhouettes.** Begin with the historic riverfront: old masonry and remodeled shopfronts, courier infrastructure and recognizable street views. Then design the southern works/stockyard district, the older northern neighborhood and newer northern trade district using the saved regional synthesis. Their ecology, routes, architecture and activities should differ.
5. **Sound, interaction and polish.** Review movement against collision, visual depth, map boundary treatment, ambient river/wind sound, prop interactions and navigation cues at normal play scale.

## World identity
The works district draws on rail, stockyards, industrial plants, shipping and labor. Historic streets draw on reused masonry and older civic architecture. The older northern neighborhood draws on school identity, music/social venues, community pride and housing history. Newer trade areas draw on later retail, homes and road development.

Use individual characters and concrete lived environments. Do not use ethnicity or addiction as a shortcut for an area's identity. Conflict, hardship and crime can be specific story material alongside residents' work, relationships and agency.

## Acceptance bar before calling Route 1 complete
- The hometown-to-city trip and return are understandable without an external explanation.
- Terrain height and walkability match what the art communicates.
- Each optional detour has a reason to exist.
- No unintended canopy/bridge/source clipping, floating props or obvious repeated hero objects.
- Main and optional interactions remain reachable through ordinary controls.
- Environment, player and creature assets share a coherent scale and material treatment.
- The route remains recognizable as Hollowtide through original palette, courier life, regional river geography and local building language.
- Transitions, state ownership and repeat visits behave consistently.

This pass establishes richer foliage, shoreline texture and ambient motion. It does not yet meet every completion criterion.
