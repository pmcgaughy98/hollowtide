# St. Joseph city sectors: verified reference for Hollowtide

Researched October 2, 2026, at Preston's request. This extends the existing St. Joseph field notes, local research reports, Hollowtide world bible and region atlas. It is a reference and design synthesis, not a claim that the city has six official sectors.

## Preston's direction and firsthand context

Preston clarified the opening geography: a fictional Hollowtide hometown inspired by Elwood leads to Route 1 at the levee entrance in his supplied screenshots; the route reaches a river bridge and then a fictional city inspired by St. Joseph, separated into recognizable sectors. Real place names in this research document identify source references, not names to use in the game. He identifies Southside with stockyards, pork processing, packing plants and other industry; downtown with historic architecture reused by current businesses; the older North End with Lafayette Fighting Irish identity, neighborhood pride and local bars; and the north/northeast with newer retail, businesses and residential development around the Country Club area. He graduated from Lafayette and describes the loss of the high school as personally meaningful.

He also reports industrial smells, uneven maintenance, theft, addiction and street/gang activity from his lived experience. Retain those as attributed observations. They are not neighborhood crime statistics, current demographic measurements, or evidence that someone's race, school or address predicts their conduct. This review does not establish a crime ranking, racial composition comparison or precise neighborhood boundaries. The artistic direction can include hardship, criminal individuals, community care and local pride through particular people and places.

## Verification and useful corrections

### 1. Southside: industry, work and a community around it

The city's Polish-community history documents packing houses and worker neighborhoods, including immigrant families living near their jobs. The Southside redevelopment plan's author describes a mix of industrial, residential and small-business uses, economic decline associated with changes in the stockyards, and a close community involved in redevelopment. The plan is from 2011–12: its proposed improvements are not proof that each one was built. [City historical study, pp. 15–18](https://www.stjosephmo.gov/DocumentCenter/View/16574/Polish-Trail-PDF), [plan author's account](https://www.cna-kc.com/portfolio/st-joseph-southside-redevelopment-plan/).

Modern pork processing is also verified. Triumph Foods' current company page describes its St. Joseph processing facility and lists its address on Stockyards Expressway. Industrial heritage and a current processing plant are different facts; do not portray every historic packing house or livestock-auction operation as still active. [Triumph Foods](https://www.triumphfoods.com/about/).

**Hollowtide proposal:** broad loading yards, rail sidings, patched masonry, vents and refrigeration doors beside modest homes, corner shops and gathering places. Work shifts, noise and a wind-dependent smell can convey the setting; the odor description comes from Preston, not measured air-quality data. Use factory deliveries, a missing shipment, a repair job or a neighbor's stolen goods as individual quests. Workers, families, shopkeepers and people in recovery should all have agency. Do not make every Southside resident a thief or every plant a ruin.

### 2. Downtown: old buildings carrying new uses

The 2018 Downtown Precise Plan identifies historic architecture and density as downtown's foundation and expressly encourages rehabilitation, reuse, mixed activities and public gathering areas. This supports Preston's architectural description; it does not demonstrate that every new business occupies a historic property. [Downtown Precise Plan, pp. 4 and 9](https://www.stjosephmo.gov/DocumentCenter/View/162/Downtown-Precise-Plan-PDF).

**Hollowtide proposal:** compact brick storefront blocks, tall upper windows, cornices, former painted signs under newer shopfronts, repaired stairs and occupied upper floors. Make it the legible civic/commercial hub: dispatch branch, equipment repair, independent shops, archive and a public square. The playable story can involve records changing hands, reopening an old shop or comparing a building's earlier use with its present tenant. Preserve signs of current life as well as age.

### 3. Older North End: Lafayette identity and mixed historic neighborhoods

Lafayette's own history records the school opening in 1917 as North High School and the Fighting Irish identity developing in 1919. Its official current site identifies it as Lafayette Middle School, and the administration page records the principal's 2026–27 middle-school appointment. The high-school institution has changed; the building remains in educational use. An abandoned or demolished school would therefore be a fictional adaptation, not the verified current condition. [School history](https://lafayette.sjsd.k12.mo.us/our-school/school-history), [current administration](https://lafayette.sjsd.k12.mo.us/our-school/administration), [current school](https://lafayette.sjsd.k12.mo.us/).

The city describes Old Town North as encompassing Hall Street and Cathedral Hill, with maintained mansions, distressed working-class houses, intact brick streets and revitalization efforts. It also describes Museum Hill as having both grand and modest residences. These named historic areas are useful references; Old Town North is not automatically the whole Lafayette catchment or an official boundary for everything locals call the North End. [Historic Neighborhoods](https://www.stjosephmo.gov/976/Historic-Neighborhoods).

**Hollowtide proposal:** a school/community landmark, team memorabilia kept by alumni, porch conversations, close residential streets, neighborhood taverns and buildings at different stages of repair. Let school pride connect generations and competing views of change. Preston's bar observations can inform social gathering places, but this research did not verify a ranking of today's popular North End bars.

For Black community references, the Black Archives Museum documents civic leadership, education, music and oral histories. This offers stronger character material than a race-to-gang association. It is citywide cultural evidence, not a demographic map of the North End. [Black Archives Museum](https://www.stjosephmuseum.org/black-archives-museum).

### 4. North/northeast retail: separate the corridor from Country Club

Country Club is an incorporated village with its own government in Andrew County. It is part of the wider St. Joseph area, not simply a name for all northern St. Joseph. The official village site lists Village Hall on North Belt Highway; Andrew County lists the village separately. [Village government](https://www.villageofcountryclubmo.gov/), [Andrew County](https://andrewcounty.org/about-us/links-of-interest/).

The city's 2013 entertainment study locates the retail corridor along Belt Highway in the east and far northeast. It places East Hills at Frederick/Belt, northeast of downtown, and discusses North Village farther along the corridor. Current property sites confirm East Hills at 3702 Frederick Avenue and the Shoppes at 5201 North Belt Highway. Do not carry the study's 2013 tenant list, occupancy descriptions or performance claims into 2026. [City study, printed Chapter 8 pp. 6–8 / PDF pp. 284–286](https://www.stjosephmo.gov/DocumentCenter/View/325/St-Joseph-Entertainment-District-Study-PDF), [East Hills](https://www.shopeasthills.com/), [North Village](https://www.theshoppesatnorthvillage.com/location-1).

**Hollowtide proposal:** a northeast retail belt with wider setbacks, larger stores, forecourts, newer infill and more spacious housing, transitioning farther north toward a Country Club-inspired settlement edge. It should not be drawn as a uniformly affluent, entirely new neighborhood. East Hills can inspire an older enclosed retail building adapting to changed use, while North Village suggests a more dispersed outdoor center. No present-day business directory or commercial logos need to be reproduced in the game.

### 5. Expansion: parks, parkways and historic residential slopes

The city's Boulevard System Master Plan describes a green network anchored by Krug Park in the north and Hyde Park in the south, connecting parks and recreation facilities. This is a spatial/ecological link through the city, not an isolated forest beyond its edge. [Boulevard System Master Plan, printed p. 17](https://www.stjosephmo.gov/DocumentCenter/View/165/BLVD-System-Master-Plan?bidId=), [current city parks page](https://stjosephmo.gov/577/Parks).

**Hollowtide proposal:** parkway paths, shaded ravines, gardens, stairways and overlooks linking neighborhoods. Add small creature habitats and quieter deliveries between urban chapters. Historic residential slopes can be a distinct subarea of downtown/North End rather than another large mandatory sector. This preserves texture without multiplying maps unnecessarily.

### 6. Bridge and riverfront geography

MoDOT's St. Joseph inset places Elwood west of the Missouri River, St. Joseph east, the US 36 crossing through the southern approaches to the historic core, stockyards farther south, and Country Club farther north. This agrees with Preston's eight supplied maps. The inset is regional road geography, not a pedestrian-access guide or a surveyed game plan. [MoDOT St. Joseph inset](https://www.modot.org/media/21427).

**Preston-confirmed Hollowtide sequence:** an Elwood-inspired fictional hometown on the west bank → the referenced levee entrance and Route 1 → river bridge → St. Joseph-inspired fictional city on the east bank. **Proposed translation:** the bridge arrives at a riverfront quarter and connects to the city street network. Use fictional Hollowtide names for the hometown, river, bridge, city and sectors. Preserve the north/south relationships while compressing walking distances. The game bridge should be designed for gameplay in Hollowtide's perspective; do not assume a real highway bridge is a suitable pedestrian template in every detail.
